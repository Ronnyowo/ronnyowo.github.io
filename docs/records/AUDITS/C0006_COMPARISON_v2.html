3. Correctness of the complete reconstructed proof

Write \(C=\binom n2\), \(a=d^{-1}\), and \(c=2k/(L+a)\). The reconstruction sets \[ b(r)=\chi(r)-c-\frac{ac}{2}\jmath(r), \qquad \jmath(r)=\mathbf 1_{\{r=1\text{ or }r=-1\}}. \] The layer has cardinality \(2k\), and \(\sum_r\jmath(r)=2\). Hence the claimed zero sum is exactly \(2k-(L+a)c=0\). Its absolute-sum estimate follows from \[ \sum_r|b(r)|\le\sum_r\chi(r)+Lc+\frac{ac}{2}\sum_r\jmath(r) =2k+(L+a)c=4k. \] This includes the possibility that the nonzero forcing at a nearest neighbor changes sign; the argument does not assume its sign.

For its cumulative sums \(u_j\), subtracting their ordinary mean produces slopes \(s_j\) summing to zero. Thus their primitive is periodic. At residues \(1,\ldots,L-1\), the Laplacian is \(s_{r+1}-s_r=b_r\). At zero it is \(s_1-s_L=b_0\), because \(u_L=0\). These check every residue, including the wraparound equation. The bound \(|s_j|\le4k\) follows from the range of the cumulative sums. Every two cycle vertices admit a path of at most \(\lfloor L/2\rfloor\le L/2\) edges, so the primitive's oscillation is at most \(2Lk\). Reflection averaging preserves the equation, evenness of the forcing, and the oscillation upper bound. Therefore its even \(h\) is fully constructed, with \[ \Delta h=b,\qquad \operatorname{osc}(h)\le2Lk. \] At zero, \(\jmath(0)=\chi(0)=0\). Evenness gives \(2[h(1)-h(0)]=-c\), and consequently \[ \Delta h(r)+a[h(0)-h(1)]\jmath(r)=\chi(r)-c. \] There is no circular use of the pair equation in obtaining this relation.

For \(H(z)=\sum_{i<j}h(z_j-z_i)\), the independent part contributes twice the pair Laplacian: each pair has two coordinate moves increasing its difference and two decreasing it. For an adjacent unordered pair \(\{i,j\}\), the two interaction moves onto one another give \(2[h(0)-h(1)]\) from that pair. For each third label \(\ell\), the remaining changes are \[ h(z_j-z_\ell)-h(z_i-z_\ell) \quad\text{and}\quad h(z_i-z_\ell)-h(z_j-z_\ell), \] whose sum is zero. Evenness justifies the orientation of each pair summand. The grouping respects the original multiplicity when several labels coincide, since each summand of \(I_n\) is indexed by a particular ordered pair. This verifies all contributions, not only configurations with distinct sites. It yields \[ G_nH=2q(B_k-Cc), \qquad G_nV_R=B_k-Cc, \qquad V_R=H/(2q). \] Bounding each pair summand separately gives \(\operatorname{osc}(V_R)\le CLk/q\).

The total rate bound \(q[2n+a n(n-1)]\) is valid because each ordered pair contributes to at most one direction on a cycle of size at least three. The stated Poisson-clock construction is therefore well-defined and nonexplosive. Conditioning on its finite clock count gives the displayed matrix exponential and the deterministic generator identity. The resulting martingale is bounded on every fixed finite horizon and right-continuous. For the usual augmentation, an event measurable at time \(s\) is measurable up to null events at each raw time \(s+h\). Multiplying the deterministic martingale identity at \(s+h\) by its indicator and sending \(h\downarrow0\) proves the asserted extension.

For the upper dyadic approximations, \(\tau_r\le\sigma_r\) and both decrease to their originals. The identity \[ \{\tau_r\le t\}=\{\tau\le 2^{-r}\lfloor2^rt\rfloor\} \] shows both that they are stopping times and that \(\mathcal F_\tau\subseteq\mathcal F_{\tau_r}\). In the finite telescoping sum, the event \(A\cap\{\tau_r\le t_j<\sigma_r\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal F_{t_j}\). Thus each increment has zero expectation after multiplication by its indicator. Boundedness on a deterministic interval containing every approximation permits dominated convergence. This proves the conditional stopped generator identity for all the specified stopping times and every initial law.

Finally, \(c\ge0\), \(\mathbb E[\sigma-\tau\mid\mathcal F_\tau]\le\delta\), and the pointwise oscillation bound give exactly the contract. If \(\delta=0\), the time interval has zero length almost surely; the same inequality holds. No gap in the reconstructed argument is filled by an unproved assertion from the author.

4. The pair constructions give the same potential

The author's pair operator \(\mathcal H\) in (1.1) has independent rates \(2q\) in each cyclic direction and additional inward rate \(2q/d\) from each of \(1,-1\). Its conductance identity (1.3) is correct: at zero the incident edge coefficient cancels the vertex weight, at each neighbor the inward edge coefficient is \(1+1/d\), and all remaining ratios are one. The two edges incident to zero are distinct also for \(L=3\).

Its forcing \(g_r=m_r(\chi(r)-c)\) has zero total and positive part summing to \(2k(1-c)\le2k\). The cumulative-current range is thus at most \(k/q\). Subtracting the weighted mean enforces periodicity of the potential increments; the edge coefficients are at least one. This proves its Poisson equation and \(Lk/q\) oscillation bound with the stated constants. Reflection averaging is legitimate because both the operator and forcing commute with reflection.

Let \(v_A\) be that final even author potential, and define \(v_R=h/(2q)\) from the reconstruction. The reconstructed equation gives \[ \mathcal H v_R =\Delta h+a[h(0)-h(1)]\jmath =\chi-c =\mathcal H v_A. \] Both constructions have value zero at residue zero, including after reflection averaging. Their difference is harmonic for \(\mathcal H\). At a point attaining its maximum, every summand in the generator applied to the difference is nonpositive. The two nearest-neighbor summands have strictly positive rates, so a zero sum forces both neighbors also to attain the maximum. Propagating around the connected cycle makes the difference constant. Its value at zero makes that constant zero. Hence \[ v_R=v_A\quad\text{at every residue}. \] This establishes equality of the actual pair potential, not merely equality of its forcing or its bound. The two finite constructions are algebraic ways to construct the same normalized function.

The author's observable is the pair average \[ V_A(z)=C^{-1}\sum_{i<j}v_A(z_j-z_i). \] The reconstruction's observable therefore satisfies the exact identity \[ V_R=C V_A. \] Consequently its generator equation, oscillation estimate, and stopped martingale are respectively the author's equations multiplied by \(C\). There is no change of clock or parameter hidden in the different normalization.

The author's full factorial identity is nevertheless an actual ingredient of its written argument and has been checked. For an undirected edge, the independent polynomial terms are \[ d(v-u)(\partial_u-\partial_v)J, \] and combining the two interaction terms gives \((v-u)^2\partial_u\partial_vJ\). The coefficients at \(u^rv^s\) of the two shifted monomials are respectively \[ (r+1)(d+s-1),\qquad(s+1)(d+r-1), \] while the unshifted coefficient is \(-d(r+s)-2rs\). This confirms (3.5), including the positive sign of the \(du\partial_v\) contribution. If \(s=0\), the first shift is absent; if \(r=0\), the second is absent. When an actual departure count in the generating polynomial is zero, its rate vanishes; no negative power needs to be assigned a probabilistic meaning.

Reindexing the first shifted term by \(\ell=\xi+\delta_x-\delta_y\) yields coefficient \(\ell_x(d+\ell_y)\) and test value \(f(\ell-\delta_x+\delta_y)\), with exactly the restriction \(\ell_x\ge1\). The other term gives its reverse counterpart. The diagonal supplies the negative terms for those rates. Summation over every \(\xi\in\Omega_2\) is a finite sum and makes this valid for every real \(f\) on that space, not just the particular potential. Summing the distinct cycle edges and using the common factor \(q/d\) gives (3.3) exactly. No specialized self-duality theorem is presumed.

The reconstruction does not reproduce that general polynomial calculation. It realizes its needed application as the identical finite sampling operation: for each occupation \(\xi\), the number of unordered index pairs with that occupation is \[ W_\xi(\eta(z))=\prod_x\binom{\eta_x(z)}{\xi_x}. \] Partitioning the sum over index pairs according to these occupations therefore identifies its observable divided by \(C\) with \(D_{n,2}f_{v_R}\). Section 2 of the reconstruction evaluates the full generator of this observable, explicitly cancels every third-label term, and obtains exactly the application of (3.3) used in the author's (3.7). Section 4 of this report proves that the potential in this application is the same function as the author's.

The direct cancellation and the coefficient calculation are two descriptions of the same aggregate averaging step. Both pair each interaction from one label to an adjacent destination label with its reverse, and both remove the resulting effects on other pair summands. The reconstruction does not replace that step by a selected-label autonomy assertion, an equilibrium estimate, a different bound, or a new mathematical premise. It proves the operative aggregate identity pointwise with all additional-label terms present. The author's stronger general-function identity is fully derived in the author proof and checked above; it is not a separate required output of the contract or brief.
