3. Exact dependency applications and microscopic estimates

C0005 is a theorem on the line, with uniformity over positions, partitions, subsets, and a test's Lipschitz bound. Both arguments preserve this scope. R (14) applies it to \[ f^{[L]}(z)=L f(z/L). \] This remains chamber-affine with the same Lipschitz constant \(D\); its directional derivatives satisfy \[ \partial_v f^{[L]}(z)=\partial_v f(z/L). \] C0005(2),(5),(6) therefore give exactly R (15)–(18): correction error \(a_nD/L\), drift error bounded by \(C_nDqd^2/L\), corrected \(p\)-jump sums bounded by \(C_{n,p}D^pq/L^p\), and coordinate covariance \(q\Gamma_d^{ij}/L^2\). Additive constants cancel. These are uniform estimates, not an unjustified finite-separation identity.

The martingale estimates in R Section 3 are valid for its initially unbounded line functions. Stopping in finite boxes, bounded drifts and quadratic-jump sums give second-moment bounds. The correction comparison then bounds exit probabilities from growing boxes. Removing those stops establishes the martingales used in (19). For the fourth moments used in Section 5, expanding the fourth power of a compensated pure-jump martingale leaves the second-, third-, and fourth-jump terms. Their absolute compensators are bounded respectively by constants times its second moment, \(L^{-1}\) times its first absolute moment, and \(L^{-2}\), because \(q=O(L^2)\). This gives the stated finite-horizon fourth-moment bound. Thus products used in bracket identification are uniformly integrable; it is not enough merely to know separate coordinate tightness.

R (21) counts full excursion durations, including a possible terminal excursion and the initial absorption period. The physical duration is \(d/q\) times the C0005 duration, and independent initiating events have rate at most \(2nq\). This yields an \(O(d(T+q^{-1}))\) lost-time bound. R (22) uses the geometric excursion count tail and that rate bound to control the greatest displacement of all initiated excursions. Its separate arbitrary-start argument is valid: on the event of no independent jump, inclusion never enlarges the occupied support, and each initially connected line component has diameter at most \(n-1\). The exceptional probability is \(O(d)\), by the independent-event compensator and the uniform absorption mean. No arbitrary-start displacement hypothesis stronger than C0005 is assumed. The one-label convention removes every absorption and split term when \(n=1\).

C0006 is applied with the exact same \(q=N^2,d=d_N,L=L_N,n\), and \(k=\lfloor\varepsilon L\rfloor\), for fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\) and sufficiently large \(N\). The two terms in its bound satisfy \[ \frac{kT}{L+1/d}\le\frac{kT}{L}, \qquad \frac{Lk}{q}=O(\varepsilon). \] Hence R (24) is uniform in all starting states. The conditional form covers bounded restarted intervals. R (25) transfers this estimate only while a line trace is in a chart with a larger coupling margin: except for vanishing clock/displacement failures, its retained-time integral is a subset of an original-circle integral on a bounded physical interval. The exceptional contribution is bounded by \(\binom n2T\). This is an actual occupation comparison and does not mistake trace time for physical time.

R (26) correctly addresses both mismatches of an equality indicator. A positive limiting distance eventually forbids discrete equality; when the limiting distance is zero, discrete inequality is contained in an arbitrarily thin positive layer under uniform path convergence. Taking \(N\to\infty\) and then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves convergence in expected time integral. Pair ordering then stabilizes at every time of distinct limiting coordinates, and finitely many pair indicators determine the weak-order cell. This proves (27). 4. Identification, localization, and the continuum normalization

The following is a mathematical check of the two arguments' all-strata identification, not reliance on a general statement that the targets “are compatible.”

For a partition refined by splitting \(B\) into \(A,C\), both proofs compute \[ \frac{p_\alpha(\pi')}{p_\alpha(\pi)} =\alpha\frac{(|A|-1)!(|C|-1)!}{(|B|-1)!}. \] Each side of the face \(u_A=u_C\) contributes once. Its unit-normal factor \(1/\sqrt2\) cancels the surface parameterization factor \(\sqrt2\). Therefore the physical splitting coefficient for each oriented subset is exactly \[ \rho^2\alpha\frac{(|A|-1)!(|C|-1)!}{(|B|-1)!}. \] These coordinates are independent block coordinates in the displayed form; ambient block cardinalities do not change the normal factor. Multiple-boundary intersections have zero surface measure at that integration stage, while their own lower-dimensional stratum integrations remain in the form sum. Thus the calculation accounts for all partitions, not only two-point collisions.

Both arguments also justify that cutoff chamber-affine functions belong to the stated closed form domain. Along a stratum's tangent directions, every incident smooth piece has the same limiting derivative as its trace. Ambient mollification therefore converges in each tangential-gradient integral, with a uniform derivative bound; further coincidences have zero measure within that stratum. Finitely many dominated-convergence arguments give form-norm convergence. The bounded integration-by-parts density then places the cutoff function in the associated generator domain.

R derives all deterministic starts from the stationary generator identity by showing \(P_t(x,\cdot)\ll m_{\alpha,n}\) for every \(t>0\), using strong Fellerness, stationarity, and full support; it then lets the initial delay decrease to zero. A instead extends the semigroup identity itself from almost every point by continuity of both sides. Both derivations are valid: the generator density is bounded, so the disappearing initial integral is bounded by its norm times the delay. Neither leaves a quasi-everywhere exception. For coordinates and their products, the split derivatives cancel in opposite orientations, and \[ L(x_ix_j)=2\rho^2\mathbf1_{\{x_i=x_j\}}, \] which yields the required covariations.

The Howitt–Warren parameters in R (6)–(7) and A III.11–III.12 coincide. The positive entries are \(\alpha/2\) times the beta factorial ratio. Consistency on the axes follows from \[ -\frac{\alpha}{2}H_{a-1} =-\frac{\alpha}{2}H_a+\frac{\alpha}{2a}; \] the origin is consistent as well. Positivity is required only when both indices are positive; the equal zero-index coefficients multiply opposite tangential derivatives and cancel. Dividing physical time rates by \(2\rho^2\) produces unit coordinate brackets. These checks match Definition 2.1, the consistency/positivity conditions, and Theorem 2.1 of Howitt–Warren. Its Proposition 8.1 supplies Borel continuation laws for the stopped-solution concatenation used in both arguments; the reconstruction's local uniqueness paragraph is an application of those named line laws, not a new circle uniqueness premise.

The localization is also substantive. A common cut in a uniformly empty arc gives nested chart margins. Within a chart, equality of lifts is exactly circle equality. Stopped line uniqueness follows by appending a line solution at the exit point and applying uniqueness to the concatenated martingale problem; bounded brackets and bounded chamber drifts justify localization and optional sampling. Conditional stopped laws then determine successive circle-chart segments. Every exit requires a fixed positive displacement, so uniform continuity forbids infinitely many exits on a finite horizon.

For limiting martingale identities, both proofs avoid an unsupported assumption about exit-time continuity. Truncated hitting times are monotone in a radius, so they have only countably many discontinuities pathwise. Fubini gives deterministic good radii for uniform-convergence passage. R's rational-start, nested-chart passage is consistent with A's more explicit rational-to-stopping-time construction: approximate an entry stopping time from above by dyadic times inside a smaller ball, stop additionally at its exit, and use the positive margin and bounded local integrals. These are the same local martingale identities, and conditional identities pass by bounded cylinder tests and a monotone class. There is no assumption that a subsequential limit is Markov before uniqueness is established.

The reference-form assertions used here match the partition recursion, equation (3.6), smooth core, and Theorem 3.3 of Le Jan–Raimond. Brownian one-point motion satisfies the paper's nonpolar-point condition. The displayed boundary computation, rather than the Howitt–Warren closing discussion about the Le Jan–Raimond family, identifies the exact target.

5. Differing derivations and mechanism comparison

The accepted brief contains the exact contract and no strategy hints. The substantive mechanism present in A is: uniform line correctors and their trace expansion; common-cut transfer and de-tracing; positive-layer replacement of all collision data; the complete chamber-affine martingale problem and target identification; exact sampling without replacement; polynomial density and uniform mass resolvents; C0002 path transfer. R implements each of these components. The following differences require more than noticing the same final formulas.

Circle trace versus local original-path coupling. A I.2–I.5 constructs the circle trace and proves rate-weighted comparison of its generators with line correctors. R Sections 3–5 works first with the line trace and then couples original line and circle chains up to a chart margin. It does not reproduce A's global circle-trace generator as an intermediate object. This is a different localization implementation of the same corrector/excursion mechanism, not an appeal to a new transfer theorem.

The critical rate-weighted step in A has been checked directly here; it is not certified solely because R reaches the same limit. If \(m=\lfloor h-n-2\rfloor\), a chart-crossing excursion needs at least \(m\) independent events. On the failure event both coupled marginal counts satisfy the geometric-tail bound. Circle corrected increments are \(O(DL)\); line increments are \(O(D(1+J))\). Consequently their expectation difference is \(O(DLr^m)\), because \(m=O(L)\). The analogous product bound is \(O(L^2r^m)\), and the \(p\)-moment bound is \(O(D^pL^pr^m)\). Summing the \(2n\) initiating attempts and then scaling gives precisely the errors \(O(qr^m)\) in A I.4.3–I.4.5. For fixed chart margin, \(m\) is proportional to \(L\), \(r\le1/4\), and \(q=O(L^2)\), so these errors vanish after acceleration. The stopped line/circle coupling used by R relies on the same rare-crossing estimate over \(O(qT)\) attempts. Thus no rate factor is lost in either implementation.

Trace product integrability. A II.4 uses bounded globally defined cut-lift functions on the finite circle and their product generator. R Section 5 uses unbounded line correctors stopped in a bounded chart and checks fourth moments of compensated martingales. Section 3 above verifies its compensator argument. Both give uniform integrability sufficient for the same bracket identity; R is not relying only on formal convergence of quadratic variations.

Exact sampling identity. A IV.2 proves a binomial-coefficient intertwining on occupancies by extracting a generating-function coefficient. R Section 6 proves it by an involution on ordered samples of distinct labels. Their sampling operators agree on symmetric tests: a tuple with occupation sample \(k\) has \[ n!\prod_x\binom{\eta_x}{k_x} \] ordered distinct-label realizations, and \((N)_n=n!\binom Nn\). Hence R's \(J_{N,n}\) is exactly A's \(D_{N,n}\) after passing to occupancies. R's selected–unselected interaction contributions cancel with the sample in which the two labels are exchanged in the selected slot; symmetric graph rates agree and the test increments have opposite signs. Selected–selected terms give the \(n\)-label generator. This proves the same algebraic identity as A IV.2.4, including arbitrary functions after symmetrization. It does not assume autonomous physical tags. The repeated-index probability and resulting uniform \(n(n-1)\|f\|_\infty/N\) error agree.

Fixed-time versus compact-time uniform semigroup convergence. A IV.1 proves uniformity also in \(t\) on bounded intervals. R (32) proves only fixed-\(t\), all-start uniformity by compactness of starting positions and prescribed-start path convergence. For the exact C0002 hypothesis this is sufficient: the supremum of the fixed-time errors is bounded by \(2\|g\|_\infty\), and integrating it against \(e^{-\lambda t}\) gives the required uniform resolvent error by dominated convergence. R therefore proves every required output without claiming A's stronger intermediate compact-time estimate. No tightness conclusion is inferred from fixed-time convergence alone.

Target mass continuity. A IV.3 expands the fourth moment using the four-point polynomial semigroup and gives a dyadic continuity proof. R (13) uses the stipulated random-kernel representation and Jensen, followed by the usual moment continuity criterion. Its inequality can be checked at each deterministic measure \(\mu\) before taking any conditional expectation: \[ \left|\int (K\varphi(x)-\varphi(x))\,\mu(dx)\right|^4 \le \int\!\int |\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)|^4K(x,dy)\,\mu(dx). \] Taking expectation uses only the one-point Brownian marginal and gives \(12\rho^4\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi)^4t^2\), uniformly in deterministic \(\mu\). The Markov property then evaluates this deterministic transition bound at \(\mu_s\). It does not substitute a random atom into a pointwise almost-sure identity with an uncontrolled exceptional set. A countable dense test family and compactness give a continuous mass version. Thus this alternative supplies exactly the continuous-realization hypothesis of C0002.
