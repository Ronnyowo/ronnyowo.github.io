AC-003-19: random evaluation and measurability

Evidence: CONTRACT lines 6–12; PROOF lines 13–22 and 52–69.

The objects evaluated at random states are deterministic continuous functions, including \(g_i\), \(R_\lambda g\), and \(P_u g\). Their compositions with measurable random variables are measurable. In particular, \(R_\lambda g\in C(E)\): the defining integral is a uniform limit of finite-time integrals of the strongly continuous \(C(E)\)-valued map \(t\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}P_tg\). The uniform tail bound is \(e^{-\lambda T}\|g\|_\infty/\lambda\).

For completeness, the map \((u,y)\mapsto(P_ug)(y)\) is jointly continuous. If \(u_n\to u\) and \(y_n\to y\), then \[ |(P_{u_n}g)(y_n)-(P_ug)(y)| \le \|P_{u_n}g-P_ug\|_\infty +|(P_ug)(y_n)-(P_ug)(y)|\longrightarrow0. \] Strong continuity and the contraction semigroup property supply the first limit; the Feller property supplies the second. Thus evaluating this deterministic function at \(X_s\), and integrating in \(u\), requires no independence or exceptional-set convention for a random kernel.

The index of a covering center can be chosen as the first index satisfying \(d(\pi_N(z),x_i)<\epsilon/8\). This is a deterministic function on the finite set \(S_N\), hence is measurable at a stopping time. It is not a random spatial evaluation of an evaluation-measurable random kernel.

The proof's conditional-expectation equalities are used for each fixed \(s,u,g\), with bounded measurable multipliers. It does not assert a spatially simultaneous identity by integrating pointwise almost-sure identities against a random atomic measure. The random-kernel construction in STATEMENT-v1 is neither imported as a proved dependency nor established by C0002. Therefore this supplemental PASS does not certify that construction's separate measurability requirements.

AC-003-21: uniformity and conditional optional stopping

Evidence: CONTRACT lines 10–16; PROOF lines 13–35, with the finite-state identity in lines 6–9.

The adversarial issue is whether an estimate valid at a prescribed deterministic start can fail after the microscopic process reaches another state. Here the hypothesis explicitly takes the supremum over every state in \(S_N\). After fixing \(\epsilon>0\) and \(a\in(0,1/4)\), the finite covering and uniform resolvent approximation produce a common \(\lambda\) and a common threshold \(N_0\), independent of the starting state, such that \[ 0\le h_{N,i}\le1, \qquad \|h_{N,i}-g_i\circ\pi_N\|_\infty\le a \quad(N\ge N_0) \] for every covering index \(i\). The upper bound follows directly from \(0\le g_i\le1\), conservation, and the definition \(h_{N,i}=\lambda R_\lambda^N(g_i\circ\pi_N)\).

The conditional version can be written explicitly. Let \(\mathcal F_t^N\) be the microscopic filtration, let \(\sigma\) be a bounded stopping time, and select the measurable index \(I\) with \[ d(X_N(\sigma),x_I)<\epsilon/8. \] Define the shifted first exit and its bounded truncation by \[ \tau_\sigma=\inf\{r\ge0:d(X_N(\sigma+r),X_N(\sigma))\ge\epsilon\}, \qquad \theta=\tau_\sigma\wedge\delta. \] The strong Markov property of the full chain, followed by bounded optional stopping of the discounted Dynkin martingale, gives \[ \mathbb E\left[ e^{-\lambda\theta}h_{N,I}(Z_N(\sigma+\theta)) +\lambda\int_0^\theta e^{-\lambda r}g_I(X_N(\sigma+r))\,dr \;\middle|\;\mathcal F_\sigma^N\right] =h_{N,I}(Z_N(\sigma))\le a. \] This can equivalently be proved for each of the finitely many possible values of \(I\), then multiplied by the corresponding \(\mathcal F_\sigma^N\)-measurable indicator and summed. All terms are bounded on the stopped interval. There is no optional-stopping integrability gap.

On \(\{\tau_\sigma\le\delta\}\), the distance from the selected center is at least \(7\epsilon/8\), so \(h_{N,I}(Z_N(\sigma+\tau_\sigma))\ge1-a\). Dropping the nonnegative integral and bounding the discount yields the almost-sure conditional estimate \[ \mathbb P(\tau_\sigma\le\delta\mid\mathcal F_\sigma^N) \le \frac{a e^{\lambda\delta}}{1-a}. \] The same optional-stopping identity also gives the conditional integrated cutoff bound \[ \lambda\mathbb E\left[ \int_0^\theta e^{-\lambda r}g_I(X_N(\sigma+r))\,dr \;\middle|\;\mathcal F_\sigma^N\right]\le a. \] This latter display makes the conditional integrated form explicit; it is not an estimate of any SIP collision occupation.

The constants and threshold are uniform over every microscopic state, every bounded \(\sigma\), and every initial law. The first-exit event bounds every projected increment with offset at most \(\delta\). First taking the large-\(N\) limit superior, then \(\delta\downarrow0\), and then \(a\downarrow0\), gives exactly the claimed uniform stopping-time modulus. No Markov property of the projection is required: every use of the strong Markov property is at \(Z_N\). Thus the conditional extension in PROOF line 35 is justified rather than inferred from deterministic starts alone.

AC-003-22: discrete corrections and a determining class

Evidence: CONTRACT lines 6–16; PROOF lines 6–9 and 41–71.

The contract has no discrete torus length, nearest-neighbor edge sum, sampled physical index, particle polynomial, or diagonal-subtraction formula. Its input is an arbitrary finite-state generator and a supplied full-state resolvent convergence hypothesis. Neither a finite-circle multiplicity nor a sampled-index correction enters its argument. Checking those model identities would require inputs outside this audit and is not licensed by a PASS here.

The analogous law-determination issue is applicable. The identity \[ A_N R_\lambda^N(g\circ\pi_N) =\lambda R_\lambda^N(g\circ\pi_N)-g\circ\pi_N \] alone would not identify a limit. The proof additionally uses its martingale consequence for every \(\lambda>0\) and every \(g\in C(E)\), with all continuous finite-coordinate multipliers from the past. Its passage to a limiting martingale has a uniform bound on a horizon \(T\): the absolute value is at most \[ \frac{2\|g\|_\infty}{\lambda}+2T\|g\|_\infty. \] Uniform resolvent approximation and convergence to continuous paths therefore justify convergence of the multiplied expectations. The usual monotone-class extension yields the natural-filtration martingale claimed in PROOF lines 46–50.

The proof then verifies determination, rather than merely naming the class. For fixed \(s\), bounded \(\mathcal F_s^X\)-measurable \(H\), and \(g\in C(E)\), define \[ b(u)=\mathbb E[H g(X_{s+u})]-\mathbb E[H(P_ug)(X_s)]. \] It is bounded and continuous, by continuity of the limiting paths and strong continuity of the semigroup. The discounted martingale identity gives \[ \int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda u}b(u)\,du=0 \qquad(\lambda>0). \] Taking \(\lambda=m\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\) and substituting \(v=e^{-u}\) gives \[ \int_0^1 v^{m-1}b(-\log v)\,dv=0. \] The value of the bounded integrand at \(v=0\) can be assigned arbitrarily. Polynomial density implies that its finite signed measure is zero, hence \(b\) is zero almost everywhere on \([0,\infty)\). Continuity gives \(b(u)=0\) for every \(u\). Varying \(H\) proves the conditional transition identity for every fixed time pair and every continuous test function.

Continuous test functions determine probability measures on compact metric \(E\); one may use a countable uniformly dense subset of \(C(E)\) to make the conditional-measure identification simultaneous when needed. Repeated conditioning determines all finite-dimensional laws, and continuous paths are determined by their values on a fixed countable dense time set. Thus this is a determining resolvent-martingale class, not a finite collection of exact author identities. No assertion about an SIP all-orders generator class is supplied or implied.
