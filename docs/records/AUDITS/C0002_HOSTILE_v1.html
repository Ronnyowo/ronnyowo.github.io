Mathematical checks

1. Quantifiers, constants, and exit bound

For each fixed positive discount \(\lambda\) and \(g\in C(E)\), finite-state resolvent calculus gives \[ A_N R_\lambda^N(g\circ\pi_N) =\lambda R_\lambda^N(g\circ\pi_N)-g\circ\pi_N. \] There is no missing factor of \(\lambda\) in the proof: when \(h_{N,i}=\lambda R_\lambda^N(g_i\circ\pi_N)\), its discounted drift is \[ -\lambda e^{-\lambda t}g_i(X_N(t)). \] Positivity of the transition semigroup gives \(h_{N,i}\geq 0\), so the discounted process is a nonnegative supermartingale.

For each \(\varepsilon>0\), compactness yields a finite cover by the required balls. A suitable cutoff is \[ g_i(y)=\min\!\left(1,\max\!\left(0,\frac{4d(y,x_i)}{\varepsilon}-1\right)\right). \] This construction is valid in an arbitrary metric space, including when a level set is empty. Strong continuity and the contraction bound imply \[ \|\lambda R_\lambda g_i-g_i\|_\infty\longrightarrow0. \] For a prescribed \(a\in(0,1/4)\), first choose one sufficiently large \(\lambda\) for the finite family, with this error at most \(a/2\). Then choose one sufficiently large \(N\), with the microscopic approximation error after multiplication by \(\lambda\) at most \(a/2\). Thus the proof's bound (2) follows using precisely the contract's quantifiers. No uniformity over an infinite family of cutoffs or over \(\lambda\) is presumed.

For a start \(z\), choose a cover center with \(d(\pi_N(z),x_i)<\varepsilon/8\). The starting cutoff is zero, hence \(h_{N,i}(z)\leq a\). At the first exit from the open \(\varepsilon\)-ball about \(\pi_N(z)\), the distance to that center is at least \(7\varepsilon/8\); thus \(g_i=1\) and \(h_{N,i}\geq1-a\). A finite-state continuous-time chain with a finite generator is nonexplosive. Its exit time is a microscopic stopping time, and an exit before a finite horizon is attained. Optional stopping at the bounded time \(\tau\wedge\delta\) therefore yields exactly \[ (1-a)e^{-\lambda\delta}\mathbb P_z(\tau\leq\delta) \leq h_{N,i}(z)\leq a. \]

The order of limits is essential and is handled correctly. For each fixed \(a\), \(\lambda\) and the lower bound on \(N\) are fixed before \(\delta\downarrow0\). Consequently \[ \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty} \sup_{z\in S_N} \mathbb P_z\!\left(\sup_{t\leq\delta}d(X_N(t),X_N(0)) \geq\varepsilon\right) \leq\frac{a}{1-a}. \] Letting \(a\downarrow0\) proves (4). No rate of resolvent convergence is used. Empty exit sets and chains without transitions cause no difficulty.

2. Microscopic stopping times and general compact metric tightness

Write the supremum probability in (4), before taking limits, as \(p_N(\delta,\varepsilon)\). The strong Markov property of \(Z_N\) gives, for every bounded stopping time \(\sigma\) in its filtration, \[ \mathbb P\!\left( \sup_{0\leq u\leq\delta}d(X_N(\sigma+u),X_N(\sigma)) \geq\varepsilon\ \middle|\ \mathcal F_\sigma^{Z_N} \right) \leq p_N(\delta,\varepsilon). \] This inequality follows by conditioning on the full microscopic state \(Z_N(\sigma)\). It does not condition only on the projection and does not assert projected Markovianity. It is uniform in the initial state and in \(\sigma\), and implies the usual Aldous condition for all deterministic offsets in \([0,\delta]\), including offsets varying with \(N\). Projected-filtration stopping times are also microscopic-filtration stopping times.

Compact containment is automatic because every path takes values in the fixed compact space \(E\). The invoked stopping-time criterion applies to general compact metric spaces: these are Polish, and marginal tightness is automatic. Independently checked ordinary background is Aldous, Weak convergence and the general theory of processes, July 1981 draft, Section 4, conditions (4.2)–(4.3), Theorem 4.4 and the paragraph immediately following it, printed pages 25–26. That paragraph explicitly extends the criterion with marginal tightness to Polish state spaces. Aldous manuscript.

This supplies a direct metric-space justification of the proof's application; a claim that separate coordinate tightness automatically gives joint \(J_1\) tightness is not needed. The cited 1978 article is correctly identified bibliographically, but its full theorem text was not retrieved in this audit; the theorem statement was checked in the author's above manuscript instead. Aldous bibliography.

The stopping-time bound holds on every finite horizon, and the chains are defined beyond that horizon, so terminal-time offsets present no obstruction. The local Skorokhod tightness conclusion on \([0,\infty)\) follows from tightness on all finite horizons.

3. Vanishing jumps and continuous limits

Let \(q_N\) be the supremum in contract hypothesis 2. Almost surely each projected jump on every finite horizon has size at most \(q_N\), because microscopic jumps use positive off-diagonal rates and simultaneous transitions have probability zero. Finiteness of the state space prevents explosion.

The deterministic \(J_1\) assertion used in the proof is correct. If \(y_N\to y\) and the time changes give uniform error \(e_N\to0\), then at every interior jump time \(t\) of \(y\), \[ d(y(t),y(t-)) \leq 2e_N+ d\bigl(y_N(\gamma_N(t)),y_N(\gamma_N(t)-)\bigr) \leq2e_N+\sup_r d(y_N(r),y_N(r-)). \] The left-limit estimate follows by approaching \(t\) from below in the uniform bound; increasing continuous time changes preserve the corresponding left limits. Thus a positive limit jump would require positive approximating jumps. One can work on a horizon beyond the proposed jump whose endpoint is a continuity point, so no endpoint assumption is hidden. Since \(q_N\to0\), every subsequential limit is continuous. This argument is specific to \(J_1\) and does not import an invalid continuous-limit assertion from a weaker topology.

4. Passing the resolvent martingales

For fixed \(\lambda,g\), put \(f_N=R_\lambda^N(g\circ\pi_N)\) and \(f=R_\lambda g\). Positivity/contraction gives \[ \|f_N\|_\infty\leq\frac{\|g\|_\infty}{\lambda}, \qquad \|\lambda f_N-g\circ\pi_N\|_\infty\leq2\|g\|_\infty. \] Accordingly, the absolute value of (5) on \([0,T]\) is bounded by \[ \frac{2\|g\|_\infty}{\lambda}+2T\|g\|_\infty. \] These uniform deterministic bounds justify the required expectation limits.

The target resolvent \(f\) is continuous by the Feller property. Along a Skorokhod representation of a convergent subsequence with continuous limit, paths converge uniformly on compact time intervals. Uniform approximation of \(f_N\) by \(f\circ\pi_N\), uniform continuity of \(f,g\), and the preceding bounds therefore give convergence of both evaluation and integrated terms in (5). For each bounded continuous cylinder function of times at or before \(s\), the microscopic martingale identity passes to the limit. The cylinder algebra generates the natural path filtration: on continuous paths, coordinates on a countable dense set, together with approximation to any endpoint, generate all earlier coordinates. The monotone-class extension consequently proves (6) in that filtration.

Convergence at time zero gives \(X_0=x\). No density or core assertion for a smooth generator domain is being silently used; every positive resolvent parameter and every member of \(C(E)\) is available directly. Individual martingale identities can be obtained separately for each test; the argument does not require an intersection over uncountably many probability-one events.

5. Conditional Laplace identity and uniqueness of the full law

Discounting (6) is justified by integration by parts with its bounded-on-finite-horizons martingale. Given deterministic \(s\) and finite \(v\), conditional expectation gives the displayed identity before (7). The terminal term is bounded by \(e^{-\lambda v}\|f\|_\infty\). The tail of the discounted integral is bounded by \(e^{-\lambda v}\|g\|_\infty/\lambda\). Thus passage to \(v=\infty\) in conditional expectation is valid and proves (7).

For any bounded real \(\mathcal F_s^X\)-measurable \(H\), the function \[ b(u)=\mathbb E[Hg(X_{s+u})] -\mathbb E[H(P_u g)(X_s)] \] is bounded and continuous. Path continuity and bounded convergence prove the first continuity assertion; strong continuity of the contraction semigroup proves the second. Conditional identity (7) gives zero Laplace transform for every \(\lambda>0\), with Fubini justified by boundedness.

The proof's elementary uniqueness step is valid. For every integer \(m\geq1\), \[ 0=\int_0^\infty e^{-mu}b(u)\,du =\int_0^1 v^{m-1}b(-\log v)\,dv. \] Assign any value at \(v=0\). These identities annihilate every polynomial against a finite signed measure on \([0,1]\). Polynomial density forces that measure to be zero. Changing variables then gives \(b=0\) almost everywhere in \(u\), and continuity gives \(b(u)=0\) for every \(u\geq0\).

Since this holds for every bounded \(H\), it identifies the conditional transitions on continuous tests. Continuous functions determine probability measures on compact metric \(E\). More explicitly, iteration against products of continuous coordinate tests gives all target finite-dimensional distributions, and Stone–Weierstrass on each finite product \(E^m\) determines the corresponding joint measures. Continuous path laws are determined by their finite-dimensional distributions on a countable dense time set. Hence the limit law is \(Q_x\). This verifies full path-law identification rather than only unconditional one-time marginals.

The original arbitrary deterministic initial sequence was retained throughout. Every convergent subsequence has the same limit law, so tightness gives the asserted convergence for the whole sequence.
